# ADR 0018 — Sketch camera: align on entry, restore on exit

Status: accepted

## Context

Opening a sketch turns the camera to its plane and frames its content so the
user can draw. Leaving the sketch should bring back the 3D view the user had
before, otherwise every visit to a sketch loses the orientation they were
working in. Switching from one sketch to another while editing is not the same
as leaving and opening a sketch again: the view to return to is still the one
from before the first sketch of that editing session.

## Decisions

| Area             | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reserved view    | Entering a sketch stores the complete camera pose — target, distance, azimuth and polar — once per editing session. Switching sketches keeps it; only leaving the editor clears it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Entry            | The stored pose is captured after the rebuild at the sketch's timeline position is ready and before the first alignment. The camera then looks along the sketch normal and rolls so the sketch's own X axis is horizontal and its Y axis vertical, so a rotated sketch opens the way it was drawn, then frames the sketch content; an empty sketch keeps the current zoom and only centres on its plane.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Switch           | Opening another sketch while editing aligns the camera to that sketch and leaves the reserved pose untouched. A switch is not an exit followed by an entry.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Exit             | Finishing, closing or otherwise clearing the active sketch moves the camera back to the reserved pose in one transition. Manual "Orient to sketch" from the navigation cube is a deliberate view change and does not touch the reserved pose.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Turning the view | A sketch is drawn flat, so a turn of the camera pans while the view faces its plane: the sheet moves and the plane never tilts away under the gesture. The nav cube is the way out — it always turns the view — and once the camera is off the plane rotation is rotation again. The rule is one predicate, `facesPlane` in `viewport/sketchView.ts`, measured against the frame of the sketch being edited, so aligning to a face or clicking a face of the cube makes and breaks it without any state of its own. The cube tooltip states it while a sketch is open.                                                                                                 |
| Remembered view  | The camera is kept by the app, not by the document: `icancad.modelCameras` in the local storage holds one pose for each model of each project, because a model id is unique only inside its project. The pose is the whole camera — target, distance, azimuth, polar and roll — so a remembered view has both the place it was looked at and the direction it was looked from. A view is written when it comes to rest, not on every frame of a gesture, and never while the camera is still on its way between two poses: a pose passed through is nobody's view. A sketch has a view of its own that the editor gives back, so nothing is written while one is open. |
| First fit        | A model opens at the view it was left with, and a switch moves between two remembered views. A model never seen before starts from the default pose and is fitted to its content once the kernel is idle: a build delivers one body at a time, and the first of them is a corner of what is coming, so the fit waits for the rebuild to finish; it is skipped while a sketch is being edited. The fitted pose is remembered like any other. A document written by an older build loses the camera it carried when it is read.                                                                                                                                          |
| Body auto-fit    | Bodies created after the edited sketch disappear while the timeline is rolled in front of it and come back on exit. A returning body is not a new body: the automatic fit only runs when the scene goes from empty to a body that has never been shown. Fitting during the exit would start a second transition from the middle of the restore; because a fit keeps the current orientation, the camera would freeze between the sketch and the reserved view.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Sketch grid      | The grid of the active sketch is not a fixed patch: its minor step comes from the 1-2-5 series targeting about 28 screen pixels, and the lines are generated around the camera target over the visible range plus a margin. Line positions stay on absolute multiples of the step, so panning adds or removes lines without shifting the visible ones; the buffers are rebuilt when the step or a ten-step chunk changes. The world grid keeps its own fixed size because it describes the model, not the sketch being drawn.                                                                                                                                          |
| Documentation    | The rule is covered by end-to-end tests: aligning on entry, aligning on a switch, keeping the reserved pose across a switch and restoring it on exit, including when later bodies were hidden.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

## Consequences

- A sketch visit never costs the user their 3D view.
- Switching models returns to where each of them was framed, direction included, and a project
  still carries no view of its own.
- Rolling the timeline backwards and forwards does not refit the camera either, because the bodies have already been seen.
- Automation that adds bodies to an existing scene does not steal the camera; only the first body of an empty scene does.
