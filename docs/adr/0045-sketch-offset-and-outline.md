# ADR 0045 — Sketch tools: offset and outline

Status: accepted

## Context

The sketch could draw curves but not derive them. Real parts are full of repeated distances: a wall thickness, a slot a few millimetres wide, a ring around a boss. Doing that by hand means redrawing every curve, and the redrawn curve then falls out of step with the original.

`MEMO.md` groups six such operations under one Tools button and lists the N-gon generator next to the drawing tools. This ADR covers the first two, Смещение and Контур, because they share one geometry engine and one interaction: a distance set by the pointer.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Menu | A Tools button with the `SplinePointer` icon sits after the Constraints menu, as the memo asks. It lists Смещение (`StrokeLineJoinRound`) and Контур (`StrokeLineCapRound`): the icons are the two stroke joins the tools produce; the remaining four entries join the same menu later. The button shows the icon of the active tool, and the entries are disabled with an explanation when the selection holds no curves. |
| Selection | Both tools work from the selected curves, not from the first click: the selection is the input and the pointer only sets the distance. Points, the sketch origin and the axes are ignored; construction curves are allowed and the result keeps their flag. A selection that forks at one point is refused instead of guessed. |
| Chains | Selected curves are ordered into chains by the ends they share (1e-3 tolerance): open from a free end to a free end, or closed when the walk comes back. Several disjoint chains are all offset at once, each at the distance the pointer stands from it. |
| Смещение | One curve that follows the chain on the pointer's side, outside or inside. An open chain stays open; a closed chain stays closed. The distance is the pointer's distance to the chain, signed by its side. |
| Контур | A closed curve that wraps the chain like a stroke. An open chain becomes one loop: the two sides at ±d joined by caps at its ends. A closed chain becomes two loops, outside and inside, which is the ring around the shape. |
| Corners | Round by default: an arc of radius d centred on the source corner, which is the short way round. Holding Shift switches to sharp corners, where neighbouring offset curves extend to their crossing (a miter) and an open chain's caps become straight ends. Inside corners — and corners where the offset curves cross under their own ends, as at a shallow kink — are always extended to that crossing, so a nearly smooth joint never grows a stray semicircle. The style is never changed silently: a round result is not retried as sharp, because a stroke that turns sharp on its own reads as a broken tool. A corner whose arc would have no length — the two ends sit on one ray from the corner, which a joint that is all but tangent produces — is met at a point instead, since a zero-sweep arc cannot be solved. |
| Walk order | Every offset run is turned to follow the chain, so a curve drawn against the walk joins at the ends it really shares. A run offset from a backwards curve is reversed, otherwise the joins would meet the wrong ends and the result would either fold or be refused. |
| Exactness | Lines, arcs and circles keep their exact offset: a shifted line, a concentric arc or circle at `r ± d`. An ellipse or a Bezier has no offset of its own family, so it is sampled, moved along its normal, and fitted back into lines, arcs and Beziers with `sketchCurvesFromPolyline`. |
| Refusals | A radius that would reach zero, or a loop that crosses itself, is not drawn, and the tool says so. The corner style stays as asked: only a result that cannot be built at that distance is refused. A closed contour whose inside loop runs out of room keeps its outside loop. |
| Folded curves | An arc closer to the stroke than the stroke is wide has no boundary of its own: its offset lands on the far side of its centre, which only crosses the neighbouring offsets. Such a curve is left out of that side of the band and the neighbours meet across it, so a stroke wider than the radius of a corner arc still builds. The join across a curve the stroke swallowed is never rounded: the corner it would be centred on is inside the stroke, and an arc of the stroke's radius there sweeps through the shape as a stray piece. A single curve offset past its own centre still continues on the other side with the same sweep, because there the mirrored arc is the parallel curve. |
| Ordinary geometry | The tools insert ordinary entities. There is no tool object, no link that a rebuild has to re-evaluate, and no format change: the source may be deleted, and the result stays. |
| Driving dimensions | The result is parametric through ordinary constraints. Each offset line gets `parallel` and `distance` = d to its source, with the side recorded; an offset circle gets its centre tied to its source and a `diameter`, because a circle is the whole chain and nothing else can say how wide it is; an offset **arc** gets only the tied centre — its radius is left to the pieces that meet it, which is what keeps the band the same width when the source moves instead of freezing the radius the arc was born with. Every round join and cap gets a `diameter` = 2d. Tying its centre to the corner of the source as well would hold it in place better, but it over-constrains the result: the pieces already fix that centre, so the tie only adds a rank to the system and turns a small edit into a set the solver refuses. Every joint is a `coincident`, and nothing else: the pieces' own dimensions plus the two coincidences at their ends already fix the angle of the joint, so a `tangent` there repeats what the set says and makes it rank-deficient. That is not a detail — a contour of a closed chain with joint tangencies took the solver eight seconds and then reported pieces that still move as fully constrained. Values are exact, not rounded to the chip: a rounded value leaves the solver a set the generated geometry cannot satisfy, and the conflict drops the whole set. Constraints are `auto: true` and deduplicated against what the sketch already says. |
| Connectivity first | A joint whose ends are within 0.05 mm of each other is closed onto one point before the constraints are made, so the loop the tool draws is the loop the sketch keeps. Joints and the distances to the source are never dropped: when the generated set cannot be solved as a whole, the class that matters least gives way and the solve is retried — diameters, then distances, then parallels, then tangencies, never a coincidence. A contour that loses a joint falls apart under the first drag, which is the one failure the operation cannot have. |
| One solve | The commit solves the generated set once, without asking which constraints are implied: the rank analysis behind that question costs a hundred milliseconds and more per call, while the solve itself is a couple of milliseconds. A relation the solver reports as implied costs a rank note, not a shape: it is kept, and dropping it would be a second guess about geometry the user can see. The result lands under the click in about fifty milliseconds. |
| One edit | Geometry and constraints are committed as one `feature/replace`, so the whole operation is one undo step. After the commit the created geometry becomes the selection and the Select tool takes over. |
| Interaction | The pointer previews the result live with the distance in a chip at the cursor. Click or Enter commits, Escape leaves the tool, Shift toggles sharp corners without moving the pointer. A press starts neither a draft nor a box; the magnet is not used, because it would pull the distance off the pointer. The preview goes the moment the click lands, so nothing is left on the canvas that the click has already answered. |
| Overlay | The sketch overlay draws a list of preview curves instead of a single one, so the offset and its ring are seen before the click. The hint that explains the tool sits above the operations dock, not on it. |
| Speed | The preview runs on every pointer move, so the loop check answers by boxes first and only tests the segments whose boxes meet; a contour of a long chain stays a few tenths of a millisecond. Its cost is the reason the miter retry is gone as well: the operation is built once. |
| Geometry module | The chain walk, the offset and the outline live in `packages/sketch/src/offset.ts`; the exact curve crossings move to `packages/sketch/src/intersect.ts`, shared with the topology that builds profiles. The app layer (`sketch/sketchOffset.ts`) owns selection filtering, the plan, and the constraints. |

## Consequences

- Offsetting a curve twice gives two independent copies, each with its own distance back to the source; the relation is between the two curves, not a repeatable tool.
- The result is one-shot geometry, not a feature that re-derives itself. Moving or turning the source keeps the contour connected and about its width; changing how a source curve bends — the radius of an arc, the length of a line — can leave the band wider or narrower there, and a large enough change leaves the set unsolvable, which the sketch reports as a conflict rather than by warping the shape. Keeping the width through any change of the source needs a dynamic contour: a feature that re-runs the offset on rebuild, which is a separate decision from this one.
- An ellipse or Bezier offset is a fitted approximation, not an exact parallel curve; its pieces carry no source distance, only the join diameters.
- A contour wider than the shape allows is degraded, not clipped: a curve the stroke swallows is left out of that side, and an inside loop that leaves no room is dropped while the outside stays. Trimming folded loops into a clean narrow ring is a later change. A closed chain whose hole is narrower than the stroke therefore comes out as one loop: the stroke fills the hole, and there is no inside boundary left to draw.
- A dimension the loop closure already implies is not left in the sketch: the committed contour has no redundant constraints, so a chip exists only where it adds a relation. The geometry keeps the shape the preview showed.
- A tangency is only added where the two pieces really meet smoothly (their analytic tangents agree); a shallow but real corner between two source curves keeps its coincidence without a false tangency. When even the structural set cannot be held, the geometry is committed on its own rather than losing the operation.
- The offset of a projected curve is ordinary geometry: the projection link stays with the source, and the result does not follow a rebuild of the body.
- The remaining memo tools (corner rounding, scissors, split, extend) reuse `intersect.ts` and the chain walk, but they change existing curves, so they need constraint remapping and are kept for their own step.
